# D6 re-argued, D11 retaken — both over codepoints

Two studies, 2026-09-02, dispositioning the two decisions the adversarial
reviews left open. Rigs: `scratchpad/cpdfa-d6/` and `scratchpad/d6roc/` (D6);
`scratchpad/cpdfa/`, now 3,482 lines (D11). Both extend the codepoint-DFA
prototype validated in [the codepoint note](2026-09-01-codepoint-alphabet.md).

## D6 — the falsification reproduced, and unmoved by the alphabet

First N dictionary words, the review's own construction:

| N | cp DFA (fwd) | byte DFA (fwd) | AC contiguous NFA | DFA/AC |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1,000 | 324,864 | 324,864 | 28,248 | **11.5×** |
| 5,000 | 1,662,208 | 1,663,232 | 139,588 | **11.9×** |

D6's "denser" claim is dead, and **D3's reversal changes it by 0.06%** — ASCII
literals are one codepoint per byte and the class count is 26 in either
alphabet. On a spread selection the gap widens to 15.0×.

### The decisive measurement: AC is slower than the engine it would accelerate

Hand-written dense scalar AC — the shape a Roc port produces — against the
codepoint DFA, 64 MiB haystacks, safe Rust with bounds-checked indexing:

| literal set | scalar AC | cp DFA | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| `[\w.+-]+@[\w-]+\.[\w.]+` | 216.4 MiB/s | 283.9 | 0.76× |
| `https?://\S+` | 235.7 | 231.1 | 1.02× |
| `ERROR\|WARN\|FATAL` | 235.5 | 347.4 | 0.68× |
| `\b(?:foo\|bar\|baz)\b` | 243.9 | 346.0 | 0.70× |
| `(?i)Sherlock` | 244.0 | 360.9 | 0.68× |
| `café\|naïve\|résumé` | 244.9 | 367.9 | 0.67× |
| `日本語\|中国語\|韓国語` | 244.8 | 385.0 | 0.64× |

0.6–0.7× in every realistic set, at 4 hits/KiB and at 0.02. AC is the **last**
rung of `regex-automata`'s ladder (`util/prefilter/mod.rs:630`) because the five
above it — `:606, :610, :614, :618, :622` — are SIMD. Roc inherits only the
bottom. Even in Rust, AC-as-prefilter collapses to the no-prefilter number past
~64 literals.

### The same loop shapes in Roc — 8 MB haystack, best of 4

| loop | table | MiB/s | vs cp DFA |
|---|---|---|---|
| cp DFA: decode + class trie + transition, 59 states | 944 B | 468 | 1.00× |
| byte-set scan + 2-byte verify | 256 B | **1,141** | 2.44× |
| naive 3-literal scan | — | 1,108 | 2.37× |
| AC, dense 256-stride, 2,000 states | 2,048,000 B | 489 | **1.04×** |
| cp DFA, 12,000 states, stride 32 | 1,536,000 B | 299 | 0.64× |
| AC, sparse edge lists + failure links, 6,718 states | 168,038 B | **31.8** | **0.068×** |

AC's dense automaton buys **4% for 2,168× the table**. Its sparse form is
**5.1× smaller per state for 9.4× slower** — against D10's rejection of sparse
tables at 3.8× for 2.8–6.4×, a worse deal. Rust's better rate (15× for 2.4–3.0×)
comes from dense-encoding the top two trie levels
(`aho-corasick/src/nfa/contiguous.rs:904`), bit-packed rows and unchecked
indexing. **The 11.6× that falsified D6 is a Rust number the port never gets.**

### The two rungs that do pay

| N literals | distinct lead bytes | cp DFA | naive | byte-set | Rabin-Karp | scalar AC |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 | 361.0 | 501.7 | **828.5** | 512.2 | 244.5 |
| 10 | 10 | 360.5 | 93.6 | 112.6 | **269.1** | 244.8 |
| 30 | 20 | 360.9 | 36.7 | 42.2 | 136.8 | **239.7** |
| 300 | 25 | 343.6 | 4.3 | 4.9 | **54.7** | 177.8 |

Byte-set scan: 2.4–6.1× for ≤3 literals with rare lead bytes, break-even ~10,
worse than naive by 30, and a **net loss (0.68×) when the lead byte is
ubiquitous** — CJK literals in CJK text. Rabin-Karp
(`aho-corasick/src/packed/rabinkarp.rs`, 168 lines) is 1.0–1.6× and the only
scanner that degrades gracefully.

### A finding that belongs to D3

The codepoint alphabet has **no 256-symbol ceiling**. D3's win is Unicode
*classes* collapsing to 3–8 symbols; a large *non-ASCII literal set* is the
opposite — one singleton class per distinct codepoint:

| set | cp classes | cp DFA | byte DFA |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1,000 CJK literals | 2,165 | **48,070,656** | 4,620,800 |
| 5,000 CJK literals (projected) | 6,413 | **367,919,104** | 13,187,584 |

28× larger, and neither is buildable under SR2. A large-literal-set problem, not
an alphabet problem, and out of v1 scope — but D3 must carry it.

## D11 — pattern IDs retaken

Extended the prototype: pattern IDs in `NS::Match { pid }` and in the DFA state
key as `DKey { pids, has_pids }`, a rule-for-rule port of `add_match_pattern_id`
(`util/determinize/state.rs:657`) **including its `PatternID::ZERO` space
optimisation**. Oracle: `meta::Regex::new_many`, comparing
`(pattern_id, start, end)`.

| suite | sets | checks | disagreements |
|---|---|---|---|
| curated multi-pattern × haystacks × offsets + `find_iter` | 15 | 7,775 | **0** |
| randomized differential fuzz, 6 seeds | 13,000 | 518,270 | **0** |
| single-pattern regression after refactor | — | 185,793 | **0** |

Negative control: dropping pattern IDs from the state key produces 1,709 of
7,775 curated disagreements and 29,965 of 60,080 fuzz checks (50%).

### The 14.1 MB retaken

Twenty `key\s*=\s*(\w+)` patterns. The review's figure reconciles exactly — and
it was **one direction**:

| | states | bytes |
|---|---|---|
| byte forward | 14,161 | 13.84 MB |
| byte reverse + `starts_for_each_pattern` | 14,423 | **14.10 MB** ← the review's number |
| **byte, both directions** | 28,584 | **27.94 MB** |
| **codepoint, both directions** | **946** | **118.2 KB** |

**242× smaller, 30× fewer states.** Determinization: **119 s byte-side against
19 ms.** Scaling is linear — N=100 is 7,143 states, 893 KB, 419 ms.

### Marginal cost at N=1: exactly zero

Ten single patterns, three modes:

| | states | table bytes | determinize ms | state-key bytes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| pattern IDs off | 415 | 9.4 KB | 10.12 | 508 / 1,234 / 288 / 566 |
| **crate encoding** | **415** | **9.4 KB** | **10.15** | **508 / 1,234 / 288 / 566** |
| naive (no ZERO opt) | 415 | 9.4 KB | 10.16 | 564 / 1,498 / 312 / 662 |

Not "small" — identical, byte for byte, and structurally so: `state.rs:657`
refuses to encode `PatternID::ZERO` when it is the only ID, so a single-pattern
key *is* a key with no pattern-ID machinery.

### Where the cost is, and that it is removable

| set | pids off | **forward only** | both (the crate) |
|---|---|---|---|
| key-set N=20 | 104.0 KB | 106.4 KB (1.02×) | 118.2 KB (1.14×) |
| key-set N=100 | 818.6 KB | 831.0 KB (1.02×) | 892.9 KB (1.09×) |
| tokenizer N=28 | 0.96 MB | **0.99 MB (1.03×)** | **2.63 MB (2.74×)** |
| unicode N=10 | 269.0 KB | **289.5 KB (1.08×)** | **1.14 MB (4.35×)** |

All of the merging-prevention lives in the **reverse** `MatchKind::All` DFA,
which does not break at first match and so accumulates genuine pattern-ID sets.
And the reverse DFA never has to answer *which* pattern matched, only whether one
did — `dfa/regex.rs:504-511` says so, and building it with pattern IDs off
validates at **0 disagreements over 107,766 checks**, with a witness hunt finding
none in 119,950 more.

### `starts_for_each_pattern`, and a correction to D5

Cheap over codepoints: +2% to +17% states, with strong start-state dedup (132
slots → 22 distinct at N=20). But **D5's amendment overstates it**: per
`dfa/regex.rs:504-511` the reverse DFA does not need SFEP at all, and the
`Anchored::Pattern` call sites that do (`meta/strategy.rs:860`) are the capture
pass — which under D5 is a PikeVM, where per-pattern starts are free NFA start
states. SFEP is required by the meta engine's full-DFA wrapper
(`meta/wrappers.rs:876`), not by D5's own three-pass shape.

### The blowup shape, for D14

Pattern-ID-independent, exponential in N, measured:

| shape | N=5 | N=6 | N=8 | pid cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `[a-t][a-z]*end` × N | 1,463 st | 8,029 st | **438,701 st / 26.8 MB / 3.1 s** | **1.00×** |
| `.*<literal>` × N | 6,007 st | 41,273 st | **2,740,293 st / 334 MB / 37 s** | 1.14× |

×5–7 per additional pattern; pattern IDs contribute 0–21%. The shape is *N
patterns that can all be live simultaneously over an unbounded class*, which is
exactly what a `RegexSet` gets pointed at. **A finished-artifact cap cannot see
2.7M states coming** — D14 needs a state-count budget checked during expansion.
Nothing here is a fold problem; it is a determinization problem.

## Caveats carried

- The Roc sparse-AC row is **synthetic** — 6,718 states, 4 edges each, no dense
  top levels, no byte classes. 31.8 MiB/s is a floor for a naive port, not a
  measurement of a good one; the honest bracket runs to Rust's 2.4–3.0×.
- A **5-in-85,010 count divergence (0.006%)** between the codepoint sweep and AC
  LeftmostFirst at 1,000 literals, reproducible at 16 MiB and absent at 4 MiB,
  **not isolated**. It does not touch the throughput ratios.
- `cjk`/`mixed` at 5,000 literals did not finish; those rows are projections that
  run 1.22–1.75× *under* measured wherever both exist.
- The unicode-set byte figures (138 KB) are **not real automata** — any set with
  a Unicode `\b` triggers the quit set (`dense.rs:1213`), so the byte DFA never
  materialises the UTF-8 states and errors on non-ASCII input.
- Rust timings were taken under machine load 4–16; absolute MiB/s moved ±40%,
  ratios were stable across three runs. Roc numbers moved <5%.
